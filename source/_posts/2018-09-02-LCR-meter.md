# Introduction

This was a hardware project that brought together ARM, a CPLD, and DDS (Direct Digital Synthesis). It's a working LCR meter built on the standard measurement theory: drive the part under test with a known sine wave, measure the voltage across it and the current through it, then work out the impedance from the amplitude and phase. The system breaks down into a few modules: a signal source, voltage and current detection, and digital processing.

**Signal source.** A CPLD drives a DDS core that generates sine waves from DC up to 500 kHz. The output covers 10 mV to 2 V in voltage mode and 10 mA to 1 A in current-source mode.

**Phase detection.** To get the phase difference between the two sine waves (voltage and current), the circuit uses a multi-slope analog-to-digital conversion technique.

**Digital processing.** An S3C6410 (ARM11) handles the digital filtering and vector decomposition that turn raw measurements into L, C, and R values.

My part was the circuit design, the CPLD logic, and the C++ user interface.

# Overview

![](overview.jpg)

* `CPLD`: communication interface, multi-slope ADC controller, SPI interface, and the DDS module.
* `Analog circuit`: measurement circuit design, amplifier stability analysis, and signal integrity (SI) analysis.
* `Software`: a `Measure` class hierarchy built on abstract base classes and virtual functions, so each measurement mode plugs in cleanly.
